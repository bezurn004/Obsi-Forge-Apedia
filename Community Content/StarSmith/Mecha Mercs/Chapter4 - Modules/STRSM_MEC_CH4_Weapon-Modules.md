# [[_Starsmith Mecha Mercs|Starsmith Mecha Mercs]] - [[_STRSM_MEC_CH4_Modules|Modules]] Weapon Modules
These are mainly offensive modules you can mount on your mech.

## Concussive Missiles
> Your missile array is armed with 5 ammo. If you [[SF_CH3_Resupply#Resupply Move Card|Resupply]] in a place where your missiles can be replenished, you may exchange any earned +supply for +ammo.
> 
> When you make a move by launching a concussive missile, suffer -1 ammo. On a hit, take +1 momentum and add +1 to your next move (not a progress move).

Concussive missiles are meant to be similar to [[SF_CH3_Secure an Advantage#Secure an Advantage Move Card|Secure an Advantage]] except through an attack.

Enemy mech got you in a bad spot? [[SF_CH3_Clash#Clash Move Card|Clash]] with concussive missiles to knock them back which gives you a chance to get back in control with an add +1 on the next move.

Need to destroy that support on the enemy starbase? [[SF_CH3_Secure an Advantage#Secure an Advantage Move Card|Secure an Advantage]] with a concussive missile to weaken the structure, and then you can [[SF_CH3_Gain Ground#Gain Ground Move Card|Gain Ground]] with add +2 (one from [[SF_CH3_Secure an Advantage#Secure an Advantage Move Card|Secure an Advantage]] and one from the missile).

## Energy Whip
> Your mech wields a chain whip that can be charged with ionized particles. When you charge your whip, roll +iron. On a hit, take up to +3 energy. On a weak hit, take up to +2. On a miss, take up to +2 but suffer -1 integrity. The whip’s max energy is 3.
> 
> When you make moves aided by this energy to attack or overcome obstacles, add +2 and suffer -1 energy.

Energy whips are powerful tools but dangerous to the user as the energy can lash out if your frame can handle it. I debated between rolling +edge (fine control) and +iron (resistance) to charge it but ultimately settled on +iron to represent your metallic frame withstanding the coursing energies.

You can obviously use your whip to deal damage in a fight, but you might also use it for other things like overloading an electronic network, setting volatile materials ablaze, or jump-starting a starship’s eidolon drive. The type of energy is up to you.

## Gatling Gun
> When you  [[SF_CH3_Secure an Advantage#Secure an Advantage Move Card|Secure an Advantage]] by laying down suppression fire and score a hit, add an additional +1 to your next move or take an additional +1 momentum.
> 
> When you [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] with a hail of bullets (decide before rolling), mark an additional progress on a hit but  [[SF_CH3_Lose Momentum#Lose Momentum Move Card|Lose Momentum]]  (-1).

The Gatling Gun is modeled on the original Gunner asset, but I felt like suppression fire was really more about pinning down the opponent to set up the next move rather than making direct progress.

You can also combo the Gatling Gun by choosing to take the extra momentum when you  [[SF_CH3_Secure an Advantage#Secure an Advantage Move Card|Secure an Advantage]] before using that momentum to [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] hard with extra bullets.

## Gravity-Shift Mace
> When you make a move to attack, damage or destroy by altering the gravity of your mace, choose the gravitational force before rolling: 2 g, 3 g, or 4 g.
> 
> If either challenge die is equal to or less than the gravitational force, count a strong hit as a strong hit with a match and a weak hit as a strong hit.
> 
> If both challenge dice are greater than the gravitational force and your action die is equal to or less than the gravitational force, you are put at a disadvantage or set off an unintended effect; count a weak hit as a miss.

This module is modeled on the original [[AST_Demolitionist|DEMOLITIONIST]] asset, but the ante is upped a bit with higher values to choose.

Narratively, you’re like using a tiny, magnetically contained singularity to alter the gravity on your mace thereby altering the force of the blow. If you miss, that singularity gets tough to control. It might destroy your mace, pull you in an unfortunate direction, or go completely off the chain and start growing into a true black hole.

## Kinetic Hammer
> When you score a hit with a [[SF_CH3_Strike|Stirke]] or [[SF_CH3_Clash#Clash Move Card|Clash]] with this hammer, mark this module as charged.
> 
> When you [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] or [[SF_CH3_Clash#Clash Move Card|Clash]] with your charged hammer, you may release the stored kinetic energy. If you release the energy on a [[SF_CH3_Strike|Stirke]], count a miss as a weak hit. If you release the energy on a [[SF_CH3_Clash#Clash Move Card|Clash]], count a weak hit as a strong hit. After you resolve the move, the hammer loses its charge.

Think of this as the mech version of Black Panther’s suit. When you successfully bop someone with the hammer, it stores that kinetic energy for later release.

Note that the charging isn’t perpetual. In other words, you can’t [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]], release the charge, and then immediately regain the charge from that same [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]]. You either gain or lose the charge, not both during the same move.

## Nanocarbon Blade
> When you [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] or [[SF_CH3_Clash#Clash Move Card|Clash]] in close quarters, add +1. On a strong hit with a match, you are unstoppable; mark progress.

This is the mech version of the [[AST_Blademaster|BLADEMASTER]] asset. Your mech is just really good with a sword.

## Omega Cannon
> When you begin charging your omega cannon, set aside a d6 set to a value of 1 known as the omega die. When you have time to line up a devastating [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] with your omega cannon, you may add +omega die and mark progress on a hit a number of additional times equal to the charge/2 (rounded up). If you do, remove the omega die after resolving the [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]].
> 
> If you make a move other than [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] while the omega cannon is charging, increase the value of the omega die by one. If the omega die is at a value of 6 when you would increase it or if the omega die increases above your mech’s integrity, the cannon overloads. Suffer -2 integrity and remove the omega die.

This weapon is a gamble. You can risk letting it charge up to its max value and absolutely annihilate a foe, but if you’re not in control for the next move (i.e. unable to [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]]), then Kaboom! It overloads on you. Sure, that may discharge on the enemy as well, so position yourself accordingly in the fiction, but you’re taking a big hit.

## Smart Missiles
> Your missile array is armed with 5 ammo. If you [[SF_CH3_Resupply#Resupply Move Card|Resupply]] in a place where your missiles can be replenished, you may exchange any earned +supply for +ammo.
>
> When you make a move by launching a smart missile, suffer -1 ammo and roll +wits as the on-board targeting systems take over.

These missiles give you a way to [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] or [[SF_CH3_Clash#Clash Move Card|Clash]] with +wits instead of +edge or +iron, but they are limited. While you could always do other moves like [[SF_CH3_Gain Ground#Gain Ground Move Card|Gain Ground]], [[SF_CH3_Secure an Advantage#Secure an Advantage Move Card|Secure an Advantage]], [[SF_CH3_React Under Fire#React Under Fire move Card|React Under Fire]], etc. with +wits, you can now narratively do so with missiles.

Need to stop that clock from filling up by taking out the enemy shuttle that’s about to escape? [[SF_CH3_Face Danger#Face Danger Move Card|Face Danger]] by launching a smart missile to home in on your target despite its distance away.

## Sniper Rail Gun
> When you have time to line up a shot and [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] from a distance with your sniper rail gun, choose one (before rolling).
> - Body shot: Add +1 and take +1 momentum on a hit.
> - Head shot: Mark progress on a hit.
> When you are hidden and score a hit with this weapon, you may remain hidden from that foe.

This weapon fires via a system of silent magnets that propel the bullet forward at tremendous speeds. Because of its silent nature, this is a preferred weapon for Predator mechs.

Staying hidden on a hit does not negate all consequences from a weak hit. Rather, it means that you’ll have to pick another consequence instead of becoming seen by that particular foe.
