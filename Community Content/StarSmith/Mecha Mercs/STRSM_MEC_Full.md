# MECHA MERCS
This supplement for the *Ironsworn: Starforged* role-playing game lets you take the helm of a massive robot fighter designed for combat. You are a mercenary whose services go to the highest bidder. Sure, it’s a dangerous job, but it pays the bills!

Inside these pages, you’ll find…
- New Truths - Choose how these mech warriors fit into your specific version of The Forge. How did the mechs evolve into the fighting machines they are now?
- New Assets - Snag the new Mech Printer module for your starship or the Wealth Management asset to add money to your game. Wealth not only allows your mercenary to earn their pay, but it also can be used in place of experience to upgrade your mech.
- Building a Mech - Learn about the four main chassis, how mech stats work, and all the modules you can use to customize your mech.
- Tracking Your Mech - The mechs are technically assets, but they function in such a way that having a separate character sheet for your mech might work better than the normal asset cards. However, both are provided. The asset cards are ready for front/back printing (low ink), and the mech character sheet can be printed as the back side of your regular character sheet to keep everything together.

Once you’ve got your mech squared away, it’s time to get stompy!

*Disclaimer: You don’t have to be a mercenary to have a mech. I just thought the two made sense together.*

# NEW TRUTHS
Before you insert mechs into your *Starforged* campaign, use these new world truths to think about how the mech fit into your unique version of The Forge.

To begin, think about how your choice for the **[[SF_CH2_TR_War|WAR]]** truth from the original *Starforged* affects the role that mechs play.

- **[[SF_CH2_TR_War#Scavenged Arms|WAR Choice 1]]**  - This version of reality supports lone mercenaries with a single powerful mech. Playing as one of these mercs puts you in a position with leverage. If a group is not willing to pay you for a job, it would be difficult for them to find another mercenary.
- **[[SF_CH2_TR_War#Guns For Hire|WAR Choice 2]]**  - In this version of The Forge, there are likely several small companies of mercenaries. You would be just a single warrior in one of these crews. This would give you a steady supply of vows in the form of jobs coming down from your commander to allow for a more episodic style of play.
- **[[SF_CH2_TR_War#Shock And War|WAR Choice 3]]** - Factions are the most likely source of mechs in this version of The Forge. You may be a single troop in a huge squadron which means you’ll have to zoom in narratively to play out how the big, sector-wide decisions are affecting you and those you care about specifically. This may lend itself to an epic fight against the system or political intrigue as much as mech combat.

Your choice for **[[SF_CH2_TR_War|WAR]]** will likely coincide with the same choice on the **MECH BUILDS** truth on the next page.

Another area to think about is the **MECH ENVIRONMENT**. Where were mechs designed to operate: land, air, or zero-g? While this has narrative consequences, it also can affect the starting modules attached to your mech at build time.

Finally, think about the **ROLE OF MERCENARIES** in your world. Are they similar to bounty hunters and given wide authority? Are they “fixers” for their corporate sponsors? Are they operating outside the confines of the law?

## QUICKSTART TRUTHS
If you’re looking for a generic world to jump into, you may consider the following as a way to fit world truths into your game.

### MECHS IN THE FORGE
Like most things, mechs were born from necessity. As the great Ironhomes needed more an more maintenance with fewer and fewer supplies available, humanity turned on itself to scavenge for survival. Because of course we did.

Raiders were always on the look-out for ships that had stopped to make repairs, and even went so far as to tow asteroids to known passages where they would blow up the asteroids forming huge fields of tiny micrometeorites. As ships came through the sector, they would more often than not have to stop to patch holes the micrometeorites left in the hull. That’s when the raiders would pounce.

To combat this, weapons were attached to worker shuttles, and the shuttles themselves became smaller and more maneuverable. As raids continued, these modified shuttles were damaged or destroyed so that pieces began to be added EVA suits as a last ditch effort at protection. Eventually, the two disparate sizes (shuttles versus EVA suits) were reconciled into the more recognizable form of the modern mech.

### PLANETARY MECHS
The next step of evolution came from the people in Ironhomes founding settlements. On the ground, there was little need for EVA action or maneuvering thrusters on the mechs. These features were eschewed for more practical components such as additional weapons, armor, sensors, or hacking equipment.

The original claim was that mechs wouldn’t be needed on the surface of a planet. Humanity would settle into their post-apocalyptic era of peace. However, with the space raiders no longer harassing them, humanity once again turned on itself to fight for the best plots of land, the most valuable resources, and the most power. Because of course we did.

*12 STARSMITH: MECHA MERCS - [[#^TOC|TOC]]*

Mechs became the go-to defenders of the homesteads because one person in a suitably armed mech could defeat a whole squadron of regular infantry. Mechs and their famous duels at high noon invoked the lawlessness and ruthlessness of the days of the cowboys. While most folk did not wish to revisit such horrid days, the law in The Forge became “Might makes right.”

### OUR CORPORATE OVERLORDS
With millennia of practice under their belts, the corporate world was not to be outdone or kept from their precious profits by lone rangers in battle suits. The potential market was not “properly monetized” according to the CEOs and board members, so one more time, humanity oppressed itself by valuing cash more than its citizens. Because of course we did.

The top robotic and military companies all joined together to standardize the building of mechs. They cut off supplies for components that did not come from them and passed laws that monopolized their the market. Why take the months to build your own mech illegally when you could just have it delivered legally to your settlement in the next trade run? Besides, shipping was free on orders that included a chassis.

On top of that, the corporations kept the best and most advanced mech components for their own private armies. Even if a settlement could afford to purchase ten mechs, they were no match for a small [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] force of two or three corporate mechs.

Outgunned and lacking the capital to compete, many settlements had no choice but to turn to one of the shady company of mercenaries that were cropping up in response to the corporate chokehold.

### MERCENARIES TO THE RESCUE?
“Fight the power” became a rallying cry across the sectors, and mecha mercs now represent the pinnacle of a movement looking to burn the system to the ground. That is, as long as they get paid.

Mercenary mech strike's quickly become famous like the theft of corporate payrolls at Vorix-II or the sabotage of a corporate flagship still being build in drydock above Cilles-IV. The common people cheer on these rebels in the desperate hope that things may one day change for the better.

# NEW ASSETS
There are two new assets in this supplement. The **[[#MECH PRINTER|Mech Printer]]** allows you to more easily build a mech, and **[[#WEALTH MANAGEMENT|Wealth Management]]** adds money as a resource that can also be used to build mechs.

## MECH PRINTER
#incomplete _asset card for Mech Printer_

Building a mech is a complex feat of engineering. If your character undertakes the task of building a mech without this printer, it could take months to finish building just the chassis if they are building it on their own. Not to mention the amount of resources it would take! In other words, make sure there is a high narrative cost to building a mech without this asset or some other in-game assistance (such as corporate backers donating the mech).

Once you have this printer, you can print a new mech directly onboard your starship. Having this asset also opens up the possibility of paying with wealth rather than experience so that you can save your experience for other assets. (More on wealth later.) On all future pages, I refer to the cost of a mech as experience because not everyone will take this asset and the Wealth Management asset together.

The second ability here gives you the same quick method of producing modules for your mech. Without this, crafting a module could take weeks of building, testing, and installing.

The third ability is a near copy of the third ability on your starship. You are forging an emotional bond to a specific mech that gets a special designation as your primary mech. This represents you getting to know the little quirks and subtle touches necessary to pilot your mech with excellence.

## WEALTH MANAGEMENT
#incomplete _asset card for Wealth Managment_

This asset is a way to separate your money (denoted as wealth) from actual resources (supply). If you want wealth to be part of your games right from the start, consider taking this asset for free.

Unlike base Ironsworn where the wealth truly was gold in a bag on your belt, remember that in Starforged you have more options for how you narratively handle wealth. It might be credits on an encrypted chit, digital funds in a bank, investments in stock portfolios, etc. Make it fit your character and their lifestyle.

I tweaked the first ability so that it can overlap with the [[AST_Mercenary|MERCENARY]] asset. That means you could get paid and still earn a higher legacy reward if you have both assets.

The first ability not only allows you to gain wealth but to leverage that wealth. For example, if you [[SF_CH3_Compel#Compel Move Card|Compel]] someone with a bribe, you can use wealth. Additionally, you can wipe out your wealth (since 6 is the max) to clear the indebted burden. That gives you an idea of the scale of wealth since six buys back your starship. On top of that, I added a fun little narrative way to spend wealth in a “game-changing investment.” What is that? I don’t know! You decide something cool to fit your game like funding research into new technology or helping to rebuild a decimated settlement.

The second ability lets you exchange between supply and wealth, but it’s always at a loss so you don’t cheese the system.

The third ability is very similar to the [[AST_Scavenger|SCAVENGER]] asset’s first ability, but they are meant to be separate based on intent. Are looking for supply or wealth? As written, you could scavenge for supplies and then search for wealth, but I’d recommend deciding on one or the other to roll, not both.

# MECH CHASSIS
The initial mech chassis is considered an asset meaning it **costs three experience** to purchase or be taken as a starting asset. For classification purposes, consider the mech asset a Command Vehicle asset because you will be able to add and break Modules.

Remember that building a mech is a complex and lengthy process. If you don’t have the **[[#MECH PRINTER|Mech Printer]]** asset, make sure your character takes an appropriate amount of time and resources effectively paying a narrative cost.

There are four chassis assets to choose from, each with a different focus for combat that corresponds to a stat. Each chassis not only has unique abilities available through their asset but also a starting module that comes installed on the chassis.
- **Zephyr** (Edge) - Built for speed and flight.
- **Gladiator** (Iron) - Built for strength and endurance.
- **Predator** (Shadow) - Built for stealth and surgical strikes.
- **Medusa** (Wits) - Built for hacking and scanning.

Most of the abilities for these chassis are taken directly from the assets Shawn created for vanilla *Starforged*. (Thanks for the generous licensing, Shawn!) I’ve pulled together multiple abilities that fit each concept I had for a chassis and added tweaks here and there. Each is unlocked for two experience like normal.

*16 STARSMITH: MECHA MERCS - [[#^TOC|TOC]]*

### ZEPHYR
The Zephyr chassis is built from the ground up for speed. It can move swiftly across the battlefield to hold strategic positions, perform hit and run attacks, and even retreat easily if need be. If you have a need for speed, this is the chassis for you.

> ### ASSET ABILITIES
> ⬢ When you [[SF_CH3_Face Danger#Face Danger Move Card|Face Danger]] or [[SF_CH3_React Under Fire#React Under Fire move Card|React Under Fire]] to navigate through hazardous skies, avoid obstacles, or evade an attack, add +1 and take +1 momentum on a hit.
> ⬡ When you [[SF_CH3_Enter the Fray#Enter The Fray Move Card|Enter The Fray]] by speeding towards a strategic position, you may reroll any dice. On a strong hit, add +1 to your next move.
> ⬡ When you fire your afterburner to [[SF_CH3_Secure an Advantage#Secure an Advantage Move Card|Secure an Advantage]] or [[SF_CH3_Gain Ground#Gain Ground Move Card|Gain Ground]] to perform a risky maneuver, you may add +integrity/2 (rounded up) and take +2 momentum on a strong hit. If you do, count a weak hit as a miss.
>
> **STARTING MODULE:** Flight System
> **FAVORED STAT:** Edge

Notice that the first ability is defensive in nature. In a fight, this can only be used when you are in a bad spot. Outside of combat, this can be used any time you [[SF_CH3_Face Danger#Face Danger Move Card|Face Danger]].

The second ability represents you getting where you need to be before your foes. Need to hold the bridge? You can make it there faster than the trudging drones coming for you. Need to make it to the artillery cannon before the gunners get there? You got this!

The third ability (taken from the [[AST_Hoverbike|HOVERBIKE]] support vehicle asset) should be seen as not stacking with the first ability. In the first ability, you’re navigating which implies a more careful approach while this ability implies reckless speed. Damn the warning lights! It’s time to GO!

*17 By Eric Bright - [[#^TOC|TOC]]*

### GLADIATOR
Nothing outlasts the Gladiator chassis (batteries not included)! This frame is designed for power and endurance. It takes a lickin’ and keep on tickin’. If you want to crush your enemies beneath your feet like parents telling their kids that Santa is a lie, you need to pilot a Gladiator.

> ### ASSET ABILITIES
> ⬢ When you [[SF_CH3_Face Danger#Face Danger Move Card|Face Danger]] or [[SF_CH3_React Under Fire#React Under Fire move Card|React Under Fire]] by taking a hit, crashing through an obstacle, or resisting forcefully, you may (after rolling) replace the value of your action die with the Gladiator’s integrity; if you do, [[SF_CH3_Lose Momentum#Lose Momentum Move Card|Lose Momentum]] (-1).
> ⬡ When you [[SF_CH3_Strike#Strike Move Card|Stirke]] or [[SF_CH3_Clash#Clash Move Card|Clash]] with brutal power by overcharging weapons or overloading melee servos, you may add +integrity/2 (rounded up). If you do, count a weak hit as a miss.
> ⬡ When you [[SF_CH3_Secure an Advantage#Secure an Advantage Move Card|Secure an Advantage]] or [[SF_CH3_Gain Ground#Gain Ground Move Card|Gain Ground]] using the staggering strength or resistance of the Gladiator, add +1 and take +1 momentum on a hit.
> 
> **STARTING MODULE:** Heavy Armor
> **FAVORED STAT:** Iron

The three abilities above really split up the original [[AST_Exosuit|EXOSUIT]] asset’s first ability. I felt the original [[AST_Exosuit|EXOSUIT]] was great if you didn’t want a deeper mech, but that first ability was very broad if you want to fine-tune. ## MECH STATS
Once you have your mech chassis picked out, you’ll next need to choose a method for utilizing stats while piloting your mech.
There are three options:
1. **Use Your Stats** - When you pilot your mech, it’s exactly the same as piloting your Command Vehicle. All your stats are the same and rolls are made like normal. Your other assets interact the same way they would with a Support Vehicle or your Command Vehicle. Give the Gladiator an integrity of six and the other chassis an integrity of five.
2. **Favored Stat Bonus** - Give the Gladiator an integrity of six and the other chassis an integrity of five. When you pilot your mech and make a move with your favored stat that utilizes the mech’s capabilities in some way, choose one option below:
	- Trust the machine: Add +1.
	- Get pumped: Take +2 momentum on a strong hit.
3. **Use Mech Stats** - When you pilot your mech, use the mech stats for all rolls except +heart. In that case, use your character’s heart stat. Since wits is used for hacking, you may use this as an alternate rule:
	- When rolling +wits by utilizing your mech’s onboard computer systems to hack, run sensors, etc., use the lower value between your character’s wits and your mech’s wits.

This is really a preference issue on how you want to play. I like using the mech stats so a character who used iron as their dump stat can go crazy body slamming Andre the Giant like Hulk Hogan in a metal body, but you may want the simplicity of not switching stats throughout gameplay.

# MECH MODULES
Just like your Command Vehicle asset, a mech can add modules to further customize your gameplay. Modules attached to a mech can be broken just like modules on the Command Vehicle. Mechs follow the exact same outcomes in [[SF_CH3_Withstand Damage#Withstand Damage Move Card|Withstand  Damage]] as your Command Vehicle does except that mechs cannot be cursed.

Modules fall into four categories (weapons, armor, sensors, and utility) which are arbitrary and mean nothing mechanically. They are simply a way to organize them.

Some modules have charges for ammo, chaff, shield energy, etc. which limit their number of uses before having to restock.

Remember that not having a module doesn’t preclude you from attempting the skill described. You just don’t get the bonuses associated with having the module installed.

### INSTALLING MODULES
Unlike normal modules, mech modules are only a single ability and each costs 2 experience points to install. Not including the module that comes pre-installed on your particular chassis, each mech has a mod load of 4 meaning you can install up to 4 more modules on your mech. Your mod load can increase to up to 6 if you took the corresponding components in the build phase.

Narratively, installing a module takes time and resources. While you can pay the 2 XP any time, consider how your character would have the time and resources to be able to install a module.

### REPLACING MODULES
Once installed, you can replace a module given enough time and resources. If you keep the replaced module intact to re-install later, spend an appropriate amount of narrative time making the switch and spending the cost for the new module. The old module can sit in your starship cargo bay waiting to be used again.

**When you want to recycle the old module**, roll +wits. On a hit, take +1 supply and gain one experience point back that must be used immediately for the new module being installed. On a miss, the old module can only be recycled. Take +1 supply.

## USING NORMAL MODULES
Many of these mech modules are just single abilities taken from the normal module assets. If you’d rather, you’re welcome to attach a regular module to your mech but it will cost the normal 3 experience points to install and then 2 more experience points each to unlock the next two abilities.

## CREATING YOUR OWN MODULES
Have other ideas for your mech? Great! With mech modules, you don’t have to think of three abilities to fill out the whole asset. Just focus on one concept and make that. If you think of an idea where there are two or three related abilities, treat each as a separate module with a requirement of having the initial module first.

Remember that you are not bound by any of these rules. They are merely guidelines to help you enjoy the excellent game of *Starforged* in a new way. If you want a wild west mech, go for it!

## MECH CHARACTER SHEET
When using the Mech Character Sheet, note that the Heart value should be the value of the pilot (your character) because the mech has no heart value of its own.

Core ability descriptions are on every character sheet.

Since each mech can have different stats based on its build or the method you chose to use for stats, I gave a blank box to fill in for the other stats. You can check any components you took while building the mech chassis and add that to the base value to arrive at your final value for each stat.

An Integrity track as well as basic progress tracks are provided.

There is a small space in the center for taking additional notes.

With only a few modules having charges to use, I gave space for three sets of charges that can be used for any modules you have active. Make sure to list the name of the module associated with the charges.

Listed around the edges are places for you to put your modules. The hex to the left is to mark it as installed. That way you can switch modules without having to erase the whole thing. Just mark the appropriate hex. There is also a box to check for when the module is broken.

The character sheets can be downloaded as a separate PDF file.
