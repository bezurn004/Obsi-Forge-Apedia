# [[_Starsmith Mecha Mercs|Starsmith Mecha Mercs]] - [[_STRSM_MEC_CH1_Truths|Truths]]: Quickstart Truths
If you’re looking for a generic world to jump into, you may consider the following as a way to fit world truths into your game.

## Mechs In The Forge
Like most things, mechs were born from necessity. As the great Ironhomes needed more an more maintenance with fewer and fewer supplies available, humanity turned on itself to scavenge for survival. Because of course we did.

Raiders were always on the look-out for ships that had stopped to make **Repair**s, and even went so far as to tow asteroids to known passages where they would blow up the asteroids forming huge fields of tiny micrometeorites. As ships came through the sector, they would more often than not have to stop to patch holes the micrometeorites left in the hull. That’s when the raiders would pounce.

To combat this, weapons were attached to worker shuttles, and the shuttles themselves became smaller and more maneuverable. As raids continued, these modified shuttles were damaged or destroyed so that pieces began to be added EVA suits as a last ditch effort at protection. Eventually, the two disparate sizes (shuttles versus EVA suits) were reconciled into the more recognizable form of the modern mech.

## Planetary Mechs
The next step of evolution came from the people in Ironhomes founding settlements. On the ground, there was little need for EVA action or maneuvering thrusters on the mechs. These features were eschewed for more practical components such as additional weapons, armor, sensors, or hacking equipment.

The original claim was that mechs wouldn’t be needed on the surface of a planet. Humanity would settle into their post-apocalyptic era of peace. However, with the space raiders no longer harassing them, humanity once again turned on itself to fight for the best plots of land, the most valuable resources, and the most power. Because of course we did.

Mechs became the go-to defenders of the homesteads because one person in a suitably armed mech could defeat a whole squadron of regular infantry. Mechs and their famous duels at high noon invoked the lawlessness and ruthlessness of the days of the cowboys. While most folk did not wish to revisit such horrid days, the law in The Forge became “Might makes right.”

## Our Corporate Overlords
With millennia of practice under their belts, the corporate world was not to be outdone or kept from their precious profits by lone rangers in battle suits. The potential market was not “properly monetized” according to the CEOs and board members, so one more time, humanity oppressed itself by valuing cash more than its citizens. Because of course we did.

The top robotic and military companies all joined together to standardize the building of mechs. They cut off supplies for components that did not come from them and passed laws that monopolized their the market. Why take the months to build your own mech illegally when you could just have it delivered legally to your settlement in the next trade run? Besides, shipping was free on orders that included a chassis.

On top of that, the corporations kept the best and most advanced mech components for their own private armies. Even if a settlement could afford to purchase ten mechs, they were no match for a small **Strike** force of two or three corporate mechs.

Outgunned and lacking the capital to compete, many settlements had no choice but to turn to one of the shady company of mercenaries that were cropping up in response to the corporate chokehold.

## Mercenaries To The Rescue?
“Fight the power” became a rallying cry across the sectors, and mecha mercs now represent the pinnacle of a movement looking to burn the system to the ground. That is, as long as they get paid.

Mercenary mech strike's quickly become famous like the theft of corporate payrolls at Vorix-II or the sabotage of a corporate flagship still being build in drydock above Cilles-IV. The common people cheer on these rebels in the desperate hope that things may one day change for the better.
