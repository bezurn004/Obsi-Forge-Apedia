## New Truths
Before you insert mechs into your *Starforged* campaign, use these new world truths to think about how the mech fit into your unique version of The Forge.

To begin, think about how your choice for the **[[SF_CH2_TR_War|WAR]]** truth from the original *Starforged* affects the role that mechs play.

- **[[SF_CH2_TR_War#Scavenged Arms|WAR Choice 1]]**  - This version of reality supports lone mercenaries with a single powerful mech. Playing as one of these mercs puts you in a position with leverage. If a group is not willing to pay you for a job, it would be difficult for them to find another mercenary.
- **[[SF_CH2_TR_War#Guns For Hire|WAR Choice 2]]**  - In this version of The Forge, there are likely several small companies of mercenaries. You would be just a single warrior in one of these crews. This would give you a steady supply of vows in the form of jobs coming down from your commander to allow for a more episodic style of play.
- **[[SF_CH2_TR_War#Shock And War|WAR Choice 3]]** - Factions are the most likely source of mechs in this version of The Forge. You may be a single troop in a huge squadron which means you’ll have to zoom in narratively to play out how the big, sector-wide decisions are affecting you and those you care about specifically. This may lend itself to an epic fight against the system or political intrigue as much as mech combat.

Your choice for **[[SF_CH2_TR_War|WAR]]** will likely coincide with the same choice on the **MECH BUILDS** truth on the next page.

Another area to think about is the **MECH ENVIRONMENT**. Where were mechs designed to operate: land, air, or zero-g? While this has narrative consequences, it also can affect the starting modules attached to your mech at build time.

Finally, think about the **ROLE OF MERCENARIES** in your world. Are they similar to bounty hunters and given wide authority? Are they “fixers” for their corporate sponsors? Are they operating outside the confines of the law?
