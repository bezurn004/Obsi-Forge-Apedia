# [[_Ancient Wonders\|Ancient Wonders]] - [[_ANWD_CH4_Alien-Megastructures\|Alien Megastructures]]: Ringworlds
> **Stanford Torus — Type 1 megastructure**
> **Bishop Ring — Type 2 megastructure**
> **Stellar Ringworld — Type 3 megastructure**
> Massive, circular habitats made to simulate a natural planet.

Ringworlds are circular, artificial habitats ranging in size from orbital stations to circling entire stars. There are three versions of Ringworlds, each one larger than the next: ==**Stanford torus**==, which are considered to be colossal settlements; ==**Bishop Rings**==, which are planet-sized; and ==**stellar ringworlds**==, which are large enough to surround a stellar object.

## Stanford Torus
A mixture of both settlement and artificial world, a Standford torus is the smallest of the ringworlds, though many debate its categorization as such given its configuration. This variant of ringworld is roofed and considerably smaller, with a capacity to house thousands of humans.

When you explore the surface of a Stanford torus, roll ==**>Vital World**== to uncover its terrain and possible human habitation ("Simple" biome diversity with no ash and volcanic biomes; page 80).

Stanford torus make its diurnal cycle with a vast mirror at the center that reflects the light of the stellar object.

### Oracle: Composition
Use this oracle to determine the quantity of Stanford torus. If there are multiple, **Ask the Oracle** (50/50) if they are linked to each other.

## Bishop Ring
Planetary in scale, Bishop Rings either encircle a world or float in the vacuum of deep space. When you explore the surface of these megastructures, roll ==**>Vital World**== to uncover its terrains and possible human habitation (no ash and volcanic biomes; page 80).

Bishop Rings feature a diurnal cycle relative to the stellar object and the planet's shadow. If they don't orbit a planet, they use a mirror at the center to reflect the sunlight.

### Oracle: Anchoring Method
Use this oracle to identify how the Bishop Ring is kept in position.

### Hoopworld
When you discover a Bishop Ring, ***Ask the Oracle*** (small chance) if it is a hoopworld. These are toroid-shaped variants and have two rotating axes: a major one that spins the whole megastructure and a minor one that rotates the habitable surface to create gravity with centrifugal force.

## Stellar Ringworld
These gargantuan and awe-inspiring structures encircle an entire star. Solar systems containing stellar ringworlds have no other planetary bodies within them. Assumedly, all planetary bodies were wholly consumed in the development of these enormous habitats. Stellar ringworlds create a diurnal cycle by blocking the light of the stellar object with enormous panels that orbit faster than the main megastructure.

## Oracle Quest
_An explorer from the ==**>Settlement**== (reroll the "Deep space" result; RG, pg 54 or SI, pg 164) in which you now reside has arrived with news of a sighting of a >Stanford Torus drifting in deep space. According to the explorer's calculations, the megastructure's trajectory will soon cause it to collide with this community's home. Several propose to avoid the threat by making an exodus and establishing another settlement. However, the overseers who commission you claim that it is possible to travel to the ringworld, find the ==**>Descriptor**== + ==**Focus**== (pg 13), and thereby divert or destroy it. Who will accompany you on this expedition? What do you expect to find in this wandering, lost world?_

## When Discovered

### Solar System Based

**Stanford Torus and Bishop Ring** — Roll ==***>Planetary Orbit***== (pg 80) to determine in which orbit the megastructure resides.
Then, **Ask the Oracle** (50/50) if a world hosts it.
- If yes: Mark the ringworld in the orbit along with the world that hosts it. When you determine the number of planets in the solar system, reduce the roll result by one (to a minimum of one).
- If no: The megastructure is in deep space. Mark the ringworld in the orbit. No planets may be placed there.

**Stellar Ringworld** — Roll ==***>Planetary Orbit***== (pg 22) to determine which orbit the megastructure resides in and from where it encircles the stellar object. No planets may be placed in this solar system.
- 
### Sector Based

**Stanford Torus and Bishop Ring** — Roll ==**>Coordinates**== (pg 40) to determine where the megastructure is located and mark it there. Then, **Ask the Oracle** (50/50) whether there is a planet at its location. If yes, mark the world at the same position as it hosts the ringworld. If no, the megastructure is in deep space.

**Stellar Ringworld** — Mark it at the center of the sector, along with the stellar object that hosts it.
