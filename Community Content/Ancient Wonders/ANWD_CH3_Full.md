# GENERATING PLANETS
This chapter expands the Planets section of Chapter 5 in ***Starforged*** by providing an extensive dashboard to generate these celestial bodies. All of these oracles aim to prioritize narrative values to describe worlds and most of the tables are optional for that purpose. You can record all these values in the World Worksheet that comes with the *Ancient Wonders Playkit*.

The *Observed From Space* oracle has been removed from these guidelines and has been replaced with the *Planetary Attributes* oracle, which provides a more specific overview of the planet from space.

### SIMPLE PLANET GENERATION
To generate a planet without detailing it, roll on the *Planetary Class* oracle. This might be enough for you. However, if you wish to add more nuances to this place, make a roll on the *Descriptor* table on page 13 and envision how that aspect defines the nature of the planet or a specific planetside location.

If you wish to add more details to this planet as you go on your adventure, you may use any other oracle to determine features, such as *Planetary Attributes*, *Orbital Bodies*, *Atmosphere*, *Life*, *Settlements*, *Biome Diversity*, and *Surface Feature*. If these details are not relevant to your story, only use the oracles that might have an impact on the fiction or that fulfill your narrative interests.

### DETAILED PLANET GENERATION
To generate a detailed planet, you may start with the core planetary oracles, such as the *Planetary Attributes* one, which determines size, density, and gravity, *Orbital Features*, and *Planetary Class*. This will give you a picture of what you see and detect at a distance.

Once you approach or collect data from the planet, roll on the tables of the corresponding planetary class—*Atmosphere* and *Biome Diversity*—for further details. As you collect information in advance of the planet or explore it, take note of its biomes. 

Then, as you undertake expeditions and uncover more of the planet, roll on the *Life*, *Settlements*, and *Surface Feature* oracles to reveal more about the location. If you get a "Transition into a new >Biome" result while exploring the world of a single biome, reroll. Otherwise, choose or roll a new biome.

You may also have a more complete picture of a planet if you have prior information about it. In that case, roll to determine its features as appropriate to the fictional circumstances and note them on your sector sheet.

Ultimately, whatever the method of planet generation used, you will create a galaxy filled with planets that are as diverse as its sites and residents.

# EXPLORING UNDERWATER ENVIRONMENTS
The ocean's depths are another opportunity to explore the rich variety of the galaxy's environments. Given its obfuscating nature, many argue that the deepest mysteries await us not in the void, but in the murky profundity of the sea. During your adventures, you may be compelled to traverse these locations to reach a submerged site or locate an object important to your quest.

In the ==open sea== you will face the vast and seeming emptiness of the watery void. On the sea bed, the land will provide you with the familiarity of solid ground, though not without its potential dangers.

Apart from using the *Features* table to inspire the details of a waypoint, you may use...
- the *Perils* table for when you face a cost or conflict
- the *Opportunities* table for when you encounter a beneficial encounter or event.

When you undertake an expedition to the depths of the sea, determine the rank appropriate to the fictional circumstances—the distance or scope, threats, and your readiness. If your journey is marked by vertical exploration, depth is a major factor, or your objective is located on the bottom of the sea, use the planet or moons size as the basis for determining the rank of your journey, as this may affect the depth of an alien sea.

## EXOTIC WATERS
While humanity has grown accustomed to the presence of oceans of liquid water, the variety of planetary environments easily permits the existence of seas of different fluids. If you wish to determine the type of liquid contained in the ocean of a world, take inspiration from the planetary class and its features. For example, the hydrocarbon biome of a chemical world may consist entirely of free-flowing oil, while the seas of a living world may constitute a variety of acids and bile.

## THREATS UNDERWATER
As underwater locations are some of the least hospitable environments for human life, prolonged stays in these places usually lead to inevitable danger.

To represent these growing pressures, add a tension clock to track a threat. When you suffer a cost through failure, delay, or inaction, you may advance the clock to show the threat drawing closer. The Lurking Threat table offers inspiration for these scenarios, including what signs you encounter as the tension clock advances and what occurs when the clock fills.

To learn more about tension clocks, see page 238 of the ***Starforged*** rulebook.

## DEPTH LEVELS
When exploring any sizable body of water, there are two major factors to consider: light's ability to penetrate through water and your proximity to land. Both may vastly influence your experience and envisioning of these aquatic locales during your quests.

The following key terms and the illustration on the next page provide more information regarding the structure of an ocean or sea and light's influence on it.

The deeper the level of exploration underwater, the greater the environmental pressure. While it may be possible to descend and explore great depths without a vessel capable of traveling underwater, it is always safer to do so.

### ZONES
- **Open sea:** Vast expanse of water far from land
- **Continental shelf:** Gently sloping terrain that gradually descends ever deeper
- **Continental slope and rise:** Steep transition between the continental shelf and abyssal plain
- **Abyssal plain:** Vast and flat ocean floor
- **Abyssal trench:** Elongated and extremely deep chasms in the ocean floor

### LIGHT ZONES
- **Starlight:** Uppermost layer where light penetrates
- **Twilight:** Dimly lit region characterized by fading light
- **Abyssal:** Deep region of high pressure and total darkness
- **Trench:** Oceanic trenches of extreme depths and immense pressure

## ZONE ENCOUNTER RANKS
As you descend into the unfathomable darkness of the sea, the environment gets more oppressive and dangerous; the peril and urgency increase. Deeper underwater zones offer riskier encounters.

When you transition down into a new zone once or twice, increase the rank of the encounters in that zone (to a maximum of epic). Alternatively, you can use the following guidelines.

## ENCOUNTER RANKS PER UNDERWATER ZONE
- **Open ocean:** Expedition rank
- **Continental shelf:** Expedition rank
- **Continental slope and rise:** Expedition rank
- **Abyssal plain:** Expedition rank +1
- **Abyssal trench:** Expedition rank +2

## GRAVITIES
- ==**None**==: This celestial body has no gravity. Mechanical aids are required to traverse its environment.
- ==**Little**==: Gravitational forces are so feeble that they cannot provide consistent traction. Mechanical aids are recommended.
- ==**Weak**==: With enough caution and consideration, greatly reduced gravitational forces allow for major freedom of movement.
- ==**Low**==: Reduced gravity allows for significant maneuverability.
- ==**Ideal**==: Gravitational forces are at the levels to which humans naturally evolved to thrive.
- ==**High**==: Human survival can only be maintained for short durations without mechanical aids.
- ==**Severe**==: Explorers are unable to survive this level of gravity without the assistance of mechanical aids.

**Complex Worlds**
Any planet or moon with no, little, or weak gravity cannot adequately sustain a complex environment. To determine their type, roll ==***>Barren Planetary Class***== on page 43.

**Jovian Worlds**
Due to their size and density, Jovian worlds can only be planets of high gravity or greater. Reroll this result in the *Planetary Class* oracle if lower.

The distinction between a moon and a planet is that one orbits the other. Both are worlds of infinite possibilities. Feel free to apply any planet oracle to a moon as appropriate to the narrative and the fictional circumstances.

Moons may sustain environments as varied and rich as the planets they orbit. You can use the planets oracles to define them. This way you can determine, for example, if a moon is...
- Broken (shattered world)
- An environment ideal for human settlement (vital world)
- A domain that breaks the known laws of reality (anomalous world)
