# CITIES OF LOST CIVILIZATIONS
Megacities are ancient places that were once the settlements of alien civilizations. These vast sites harness incomprehensible technology and vary in their location, size, complexity, and even the material from which they are primarily made. These wonders are a rich source of lore, adventure, and narrative potential. Below are the guidelines for implementing them in your sessions.

# MEGACITIES IN YOUR CAMPAIGN
This section establishes the foundation for the addition of megacities in your campaign. The **Default Assumptions** set the ground rules by defining the key traits of these wonders, whereas the Truth provides an option to make them fit your setting.

## DEFAULT ASSUMPTIONS
The following are the default assumptions regarding megacities in the setting. These are generally true and are the key pillars for you to envision a galaxy with these wonders.

### THEIR PEOPLE
**The cities' original inhabitants are long gone.**

The precursors—alien species that originally inhabited these sites—disappeared from the galaxy a long time ago.

### THEIR CONDITION
**The megalopoleis are in a state of disrepair.**

The structures are deteriorating and damaged, the infrastructure is rotting and corrupted, the technology tends to malfunction, and the monuments are decaying and weathered.

### THEIR TECHNOLOGY
**We don't understand the science behind these wonders of engineering.**

The technology of these metropolises has left us perplexed and amazed. We can only adapt or make superficial use of them and marvel at the results.

### THEIR VALUE
**Megacities are amongst the most valuable precursor vaults in the galaxy.**

In addition to offering advanced technology and a wealth of knowledge regarding precursor societies, these ancient settlements are highly valued as potential homes for mankind.

### THEIR DANGER
**Megacities will never be entirely safe or secure homes for humanity.**

Despite adapting many of these vaults into fitting habitats, we will never be entirely safe within their confines. Their vastness and complexity allow hidden threats to emerge anytime, especially from the darkest corners and unexplored zones.

## YOUR TRUTH
The following is your truth regarding the control the precursor-made constructs exercise over the megacities. Choose one from the three options below or roll.

You may skip this decision and do it when it becomes necessary or relevant during play.

> ### KEEPERS CONTROL
> Keepers are constructs created by the precursors to maintain and guard their structures, settlements, and marvels. This truth determines how much control the keepers maintain over megacities and the likelihood of finding human habitats within them.

# MEGACITIES IN YOUR ADVENTURES
These marvels of forgotten ages might be used in your game sessions in many respects. Venturing into settlements originally inhabited by extinct alien societies offers a broad enough framework for countless premises. Powerful factions often sponsor expeditions to megacities in the hopes of obtaining significant precursor assets, but not all explorers are sent by patrons. Some go of their own accord. For what purpose?

Here are some reasons why explorers might interact with megacities in your campaign.

## TO KNOW THE PRECURSORS
The mysteries surrounding the precursors' civilizations make their original settlements irresistible places to explore. There may be no better way to learn about their societies than to study and experience the places they call home.
## TO FIND A HOME
Some take it for granted that it is better to settle in what was previously home to other intelligent life forms. It is well known that conquering these sites is not easy, but given their pre-established nature and advanced technology, those who undertake this endeavor consider the inherent risk preferable to contending with nature and building a home from scratch.

## TO ACQUIRE RESOURCES
We know that the precursors had an immeasurable capacity for creation, so it is not unreasonable to conclude that their cities may have served as storage for some of their resources. While we may not understand the properties of many of these materials, they will always be valuable.

## TO FIND RARITIES
Many of the components of megacities are a mystery when it comes to their design and function. This has made them the subject of studies by interested parties. It is not mere curiosity that drives these interested parties to seek out these technologies, but rather a desire to obtain powerful artifacts for use or for trade.

## TO CONQUER
Many know the value of megacities and offer their assistance in taking possession of one of these wonders, reclaiming it for humanity.

Conquering a megacity is a challenge, but not a vain one. The ultimate reward of conquering one of these marvels is not only honor, but the pride in gaining knowledge, resources, technology, and a home for mankind.

Whatever the motive is for delver to venture into the megacities, this has proven to be the most enriching experiences to learn about the alien societies that once conquered the known galaxy.

# EXPLORING A MEGACITY
Even if humans inhabit a megacity, many districts, streets, roads, and corners remain unexplored. Conquering one of these sites is, in fact, a task that takes more than simply settling in one of its spaces. Ultimately, whether you are in search of knowledge, resources, technology, or a home, traversing a megacity is a rich, yet perilous experience. Here are the guidelines for implementing megacity exploration into your campaign.

## INVESTIGATING A MEGACITY
The main novelty of megacities is the unraveling of a mystery pertaining to the alien society that once inhabited these sites. For this purpose, your expedition progress represents not only the physical space you traverse, but the advancement of your precursor research.

However, bear in mind that the strange and alien nature of megacities makes their features and findings impossible to be entirely deciphered in a single expedition. The amount and depth of mysteries of these sites are as rich and inexhaustible as their original inhabitants.

To carry out an investigation within a megacity while undertaking an expedition, follow these guidelines.
- When you first make progress in an expedition within a megalopolis, choose a mystery from the *Megacity Mystery* table on page 173 or envision one based on whatever circumstance drew you to the site. The mystery can be one that naturally reveals itself during your exploration or a question that you have been determined to answer.
- As you continue to make progress in your expedition and reach new segments, use the *Megacity Cipher* table on page 173 for a revealing feature, object, or encounter that provides a clue to your investigation. To provide additional details, you may use the *Story Clue* oracle on the ***Starforged Reference Guide***, page 111, for a significant revelation, or the core oracles on page 12 for an abstract prompt. Here are some examples:
	- Action + Theme
	- Action + Focus
	- Descriptor + Theme
	- Descriptor + Focus
	- Action + Descriptor + Focus
- If the cipher is too complex to interpret, you may dedicate time to gaining a deeper understanding of the clue it provides, but do not get mired in the process. Instead, leave room for unanswered questions and consider how the cipher might reframe the mystery.
- When you finish the expedition, consider the questions brought about by the mystery and the clues you have uncovered up to this point. Then, propose a conclusion. The outcome of play reveals whether your assumptions are correct or if you have been misled.

## REACHING THE MEGACITY SANCTUM
When you explore a megacity, your objective is more likely to be found in the sanctum; the most important part of your journey. The sanctum does not necessarily represent a literal place, but rather indicates the pace at which you uncover a mystery, reach another milestone in your quest, or otherwise locate your objective within a site. As you venture deeper into the unexplored districts of a megacity, the hazardous nature of the site becomes more apparent.

You arrive at the sanctum when you...
- Mark seven progress in your expedition.
- Are prompted to do so by the "Transition into the sanctum" result in the Vault Feature oracle. When this happens, envision this shortcut and how it shifts the fate of your expedition.

## USING ORACLE ARRAYS FOR MEGACITIES
Megacities are alien constructions that are fundamentally **derelicts** but are deeply pervaded by a **theme** and experienced like **vaults**. Exploring them is an experience that makes use of ==**oracle arrays**==—combinations of multiple oracles.

The use of oracle arrays is explained in Chapter 5 of the ***Starforged*** rulebook on page 384.

When you are in an access zone leading to a megacity’s interior, do the following to prepare yourself for the use of the oracle arrays that can be found on page 174.
- Treat the megastructure as a **derelict** as indicated on page 350 of the ***Starforged*** rulebook. When navigating through the megacity, you will be prompted to roll for the derelict zone. For this, reference the Settlement column on page 351 of the ***Starforged*** rulebook or page 82 of the ***Starforged Reference Guide***.
- Choose a **theme** that best fits your vision of the megalopolis. You can find the *Themes* oracles on page 100 of the ***Starforged Reference Guide***.
- A megacity is a type of **precursor vault**, so you will also use those oracles as you delve into it. Precursor Vault oracles can be found on page 94 of the ***Starforged Reference Guide***.
- Additionally, you may be prompted to use the *Story Complication* oracle on page 57 of the ***Starforged Reference Guide***.

## THREATS IN MEGACITIES
Spending extended periods in unexplored areas of alien ruins involves the risk of unseen dangers.

To represent the menaces of a ==**stalking threat**==, add a tension clock. When you suffer a cost through failure, delay, or inaction, you may advance the clock to show the threat drawing closer. The *Lurking Threat* table offers inspiration for these scenarios, including what signs you may encounter as the tension clock advances and what occurs when the clock fills.

Learn more about tension clocks on page 238 of the ***Starforged*** rulebook.

## CEILING CITY
A ceiling city is a residential site designed to hang from the upper interior surface of a megastructure, cave, or other natural formation, although it is also possible to find them on an artificial roof constructed in a planet's sky. The general form of the city tends to be that of a chandelier of a cluster of stalactites. Navigation between the structures of this marvel and the access to its interior requires a vehicle.


## CLOUD CITY
The cloud city is a megalopolis built to hover in the air at high altitudes above the surface of a world. The city itself is supported by one or many islands that contain the technology necessary for suspension, which may be huge air-filled spheres, immense rotors, or some kind of gravitic engine. At the same time, this megacity could be tethered, free-floating, or movable, which makes the permanence of its location uncertain at first sight.

## DOME CITY
Also known as an arcology, this alien settlement seems to have been designed to be self-sufficient in all the resources needed to sustain life while having the least impact on the planet. Thus, the shape of the city tend to be tall to avoid making use of the world's surface, and its interior was made with a sufficient variety of areas and zones for the inhabitants to dispense with going outside.

## MEGATALL CITY
A megatall city is a megacity designed to reach into space. Such a site would have been used to place vehicles into orbit without using external logistics. The interiors of this city are conditioned so that the atmospheric pressure remains homogeneous throughout, preserving the life within.

## MOVABLE CITY
Also known as a walking city, this grand livable residence was designed to accommodate the need for constant movement. Its motility can vary, from being pulled or pushed by other vehicles if it is small enough, to propulsion, wheels, or legs in case they are bigger.

## SEA CITY
The sea city is an artificial island made megalopolis. The structure could be an extension of a ground-based city,
an isolated city far from the land, or a movable city on the surface of a body of water. Most areas of this megacity are underwater, like an iceberg. It is also possible that this structure exists not as a single unit, but as a group of linked, mobile islands.

## UNDERGROUND CITY
Also known as a subterranean or pit city, this residential site was apparently built to obtain underground protection and cover in exchange for sunlight. Key zones of the city often have support towers that hold the weight of the surface or function as entrances to the whole structure by containing elevators or shafts which lengthen downwards as the expansion of the megacity continues deeper underground.

## UNDERWATER CITY
An underwater city is a submerged residential site composed of numerous structures that serve as the city's zones. It is assumed that this composition was made to isolate the potential accident that might occur, so if a zone is flooded, the other structures would not be affected. Likewise, to mitigate the pressure of the depths, these structures become thicker as the megacity descends, and their lights are artificial in the absence of sunlight.
