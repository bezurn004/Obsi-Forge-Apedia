# [[_Ancient Wonders\|Ancient Wonders]] - [[_ANWD_CH9_Additional-Moves|Additional Moves]]: Failing Forward
Facing hardships and overcoming them is a key part of any adventure. These are keystones of your character growth. If you would like to make failures an influential aspect of your character progression, use the following guidelines. You can introduce this mechanic into an ongoing campaign or a new one.

First, create a ==**failure track**==. This is a standard progress track with ten boxes. Then, when you make an action or progress roll and score a miss, make the following move.

## Mark Your Failure
![[ANWD_MV_Mark-Failure#Mark Failure Move Card]]
___

Don't **Mark Your Failure** if you change the outcome of a move from a miss to a hit through an asset ability or other means. Make this move only when you face the consequences of a miss.

Continue to mark ticks on your failure track as you play and score misses. When your failure track is at +6 filled progress boxes or greater, you may perform the **Learn From Your Failures** progress move.

*234 Ancient Wonders*

## Learn From Your Failures
![[ANWD_MV_Learn-From-Your-Failures#Learn From Your Failure Move Card]]
___

**Learn From Your Failures** is the progress move to resolve the impact of character's mishaps. Make this move when you are ready to leverage the misses you have marked thus far. The failure track must be at +6 progress or more.

Narratively, your character is reflecting on the consequences of their failures. Their adversities have brought them to this point. What have you learned? How do you intend to correct your ways?

When making the progress roll, consider the outcome within the narrative. On a strong hit, this is a significant turning point in your character’s story. On a weak hit, you grudgingly accept the lessons you draw from the failures, or those lessons are limited. On a miss, you haven't learned enough, or your pride prevents you from making a connection that will lead to real growth.

Whatever the outcome of the progress move, clear all progress on the failure track and return to the loop: make the **Mark Your Failure** move whenever you score a miss and **Learn from Your Failures** once your failure track is +6 or greater.

> [!tip] **Failure Track & Allies**
> The failure track is not a shared resource. If you are playing with allies, each of you has your own progress track to mark your own misses with the **Mark Your Failure** move.
> In case of making the **Aid Your Ally** move and the outcome is a miss, do whatever fits the narrative. If you are close collaborators in the action, you both **Mark Your Failure** to represent the shared learning experience.
> 
> When an ally makes the **Learn from Your Failures** move in the presence of others, envision how they participate in the scene. Is there an exchange of words? Are there reproaches for mistakes made? Does this change their bonds in any way? If so, how?
