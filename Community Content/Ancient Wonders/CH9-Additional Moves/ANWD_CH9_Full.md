# MAKING CAMP
Conducting expeditions into ancient alien structures is not a task for the average person, it is an endeavor for the self-sufficient adventurer, and no expert in the field can undertake such an enterprise without taking breaks in shelters.

Although it is possible to perform this action narratively, it is recommended for these breaks to be represented mechanically by making the following moves:

## HUNKER DOWN
**When you rest and recover at an improvised, temporary, or make-shift refuge,**- roll +supply.

**On a strong hit**, your provisions are enough to adequately sustain you, your allies, and your special companions; each may choose one. **On a weak hit**, as above, but resources or time are wasted; **Sacrifice Resources** (-1) or
**Lose Momentum** (-2).
- **Heal** and take an automatic strong hit.
- **Hearten** and take an automatic strong hit.
- **Repair** and take an automatic strong hit (in the field or underway).

**On a miss**, you take no respite. **Pay the Price**.
___

Make this move to recover amid an isolated hazardous site. When doing so, roll +supply and resolve the result as indicated by the degree of success or failure.

**On a strong hit**, you, the other players, and your special companions (see Chapter 7 on page 198) choose to make one move from the list. **On a weak hit**, you additionally make a suffer move: **Sacrifice Resources** (-1) or **Lose Momentum** (-2). If you decide to **Heal** or **Repair**, you can also apply those benefits to your regular companions.

**On a miss**, you take no rest and the situation worsens. Envision what it is that prevents you from recovering, resting, or making camp, and make the appropriate move. Perhaps it is some inner struggle that haunts your dreams, the environment making it difficult for you to find proper shelter, or the sudden appearance of a hostile denizen disrupts your peace. If in doubt, roll on the **Pay the Price** table or **Ask the Oracle** for inspiration. Depending on what you envision, play to see what happens or continue on your expedition.

**Hunker Down** is not only useful when you are undertaking an expedition into megastructures. This move also provides additional use to the supply track by increasing its value within the game system. Delvers are usually forced to find provisions that allow them to survive and recover from their travels, journeys, and combats. Always keep an eye out for supplies. They will be needed in the most dire situations.

## ESTABLISH A HAVEN
**When you secure an area while on an expedition** after doing any of the following...
- overcoming a critical obstacle
- gaining meaningful insight
- defeating a notable foe

...envision how can you make this a perpetual safe place to rest. Then, roll +wits.

**On a strong hit**, you make a reliable haven. Name it and mark the progress box in which this haven is being established.

Then, when you...
- **Hunker Down** in this haven: Add +2 and choose an additional option on a hit
- **Undertake an Expedition** from this haven: Add +1 and take +1 momentum on a hit.
- Go to the haven by...
	- Returning to it in the middle of an expedition: Clear all progress after the haven
	- Starting a new expedition route: **Set a Course** and add +2

**On a weak hit**, as above, but making this haven causes a delay or setback. Choose one.
- **Lose Momentum** (-3).
- Clear one progress box and mark the haven appropriately.

**On a miss**, this place is cursed. You find no comfort in it. **Pay the Price**.
___

The **Establish a Haven** move offers a great benefit for long-term expeditions in sites of colossal scales. Although its use is restricted to a narrow narrative condition, it provides a stable location for the characters' survival. Alternatively, the VANGUARD deed asset allows you to establish a haven by other means and gain additional boons in these refuges.

When you reach one of the listed milestones, envision how that success leads the way for you to use that waypoint as a commemoration of a significant breakthrough in your quest. Such a place can be used to recover freely and safely and as a shortcut for future routes inside an expedition site.

If you **Hunker Down** in a haven, or make any recover move there, and score a miss, you may use the haven's well-being as the cost. Make a scene for it. What happens when you try to rest? What do you encounter? How is the haven jeopardized? What do you do to protect it? You may treat the haven as having an integrity of 5 that may **Withstand Damage** as an incidental vehicle, and you may Repair it as such.

## DELVER ASSET
Those individuals that dedicate their lives to exploring abandoned structures in pursuit of material gain have come to be known by the galaxy as delvers.

The DELVER asset that comes with this supplement offers bonuses while exploring derelicts and precursor vaults.

The first ability leverages the **Hunker Down** and **Establish a Haven** move, showcasing a delver's affinity for longevity in these environments.

The second ability represents the delver's ability to identify the value and practical use of the myriad of supplies or forgotten items they may encounter.

The third ability enhances their commitment to undertaking expeditions into perilous sites to search for high-value objects.

> ### PATH
> ## DELVER
> - [x] When you **Undertake an Expedition** or **Establish a Haven** in a ruin, add +1. When you **Hunker Down** in these locations, you may choose two options on a hit instead of one. If the ruin is alien, take +1 momentum on a hit when making these moves.
> - [ ] When you **Resupply** in an abandoned ship or structure and score a hit, you leverage your practical experience scavenging these locations; take +1 supply and +1 momentum. When you **Make a Discovery**, you capitalize on your findings; mark one box on your discoveries legacy track instead of marking 2 ticks.
> - [ ] When you **Swear an Iron Vow** to hunt for a valuable item or lost artifact, reroll any dice. If the sought-after object is not human-made, take an automatic strong hit. When you **Reach a Milestone** on this quest, take +1 momentum.

# EXCHANGING WEALTH
The moves presented in this section expand upon what is conceptually covered in the **Resupply** move by incorporating wealth as a currency.

The incorporation of wealth as a monetary resource in the ***Starforged*** system is explained on page 28 of ***Sundered Isles***. However, this is the necessary information for you to use this resource with only the content present in ***Ancient Wonders***:
- You can acquire wealth with the **Taking a Hiatus** set of moves on page 230 or by selling an item using the **Exchange Wealth** move on page 229.
- You record wealth as ledger items. A ledger item may have a wealth value of 1 to 5. These ledger items may be a stockpile of trade goods, payment in the form of credits, a cache of money, a relic, or a valuable item in general.
- Wealth is spent as a tool for narrative permission. However, it is usually not an excuse for skipping moves altogether, but to actually make them. For instance, with the **Exchange Wealth** move on page 229 you may hire a repair "average service" to make the **Repair** move "At a Facility", or you can hire a guidance "average service" to **Set a Course** instead of making the **Undertake an Expedition** move.

## BROWSE THE MARKET

**When you search in a community, physical or digital, to find an asset, service, or someone interested in what you offer,** envision your approach and roll. If you...
- Bargain across potential traders: Roll +heart
- Assess the offers: Roll +wits

If you are performing the search in a dense and technologically advanced population, add +1. If you share a bond, add another +1.

**On a strong hit**, you find what you are looking for at a fair deal. If you...
- **Resupply** now, add +2
- **Exchange Wealth**, you have access to upmarket assets and services

**On a weak hit**, the search did not go as well as expected. If you...
- **Resupply** now, add +1.
- **Exchange Wealth**, you have access to upmarket assets and services but with some extra time; **Lose Momentum** (-1).

**On a miss**, you are out of luck; what you're looking for is not available and the situation worsens. **Pay the Price**.
___

Use this move if you are unsure of finding a certain asset or service in a community right before performing the **Resupply** or **Exchange Wealth** moves for acquiring the desired object. Given that **Browse the Market** grants you an extra boon for the **Resupply** move, it is a safer option to leverage this roll first, especially if you are performing the search in a fitting community or if you share a bond with it. Take **Browse the Market** as **Secure an Advantage** and **Resupply** as **Face Danger**: a hit on the first move would allow you to get an upper hand when performing the second move.

**Browse the Market** also works the other way around: it might guide you in the right direction for someone interested in an object or resource you're looking to sell. The **Exchange Wealth** move allows you to trade mechanically with wealth resources.

If you are unprepared, **Browse the Market** is a good option to make sure you find the right place within a community to overcome an obstacle. If you are looking for a rare, costly service, item, or significance resource, the move provides you the mechanical means to justify looking for it.

When you make this move, avoid jumping into the next one. Instead, envision the hunt within the community. This is your best chance of fleshing out and detailing the populated place you are now in. What is your approach? What is this place like? How do the people react to you? Use some character and settlement oracles for inspirations. Take the roll outcomes into consideration. How did the search go, and why?

___

**Exchange Wealth** is an alternative to the Resupply move for acquiring items in communities. Since there is no roll involved, this move allows for more secure acquisitions by using currency.

You may acquire something through this move that is as straightforward as a simple transaction for general upkeep (+supply). Other exchanges may have more nuances, such as hiring a service to **Repair** at a facility or at a anchor. Plausibility and Narrative Focus

When making this move, don't rush through it with a focus on the mechanical outcome. Instead, define the community well enough beforehand. This will provide enough background to justify what is plausible to find and what is not. Then, envision the circumstances. Ask yourself—or **Ask the Oracle**:
- Is it reasonable to find the service or object I am looking for in this community? If so, why?
- Is it possible to find someone interested and with the monetary capacity to purchase what I have to sell? If so, who is that?
- What is the trader or store that provides the service or item like? How does the interaction go and how is transaction carried out?

Some upmarket assets or services might require special narrative focus or implications. For instance:
- Objects of high rarity may be so valuable in certain communities that you may be perceived as an opulent individual. Will this work for you or against you? Will it bring short or long-term repercussions for your interactions with members of this settlement?
- Given their power and esoteric nature, abnormal objects may be banned in communities or have policies regulating them, so searching or trading for them may attract unwanted attention. When you ask about these items, how is your inquiry perceived? How do merchants keep the alien relics safe?
- Partnerships for your campaigns can be found in communities like any other service or item. However, the nature of these bonds requires more narrative focus. When you find a companion for your adventures, envision the scene. How do you bond with them? What makes them want to follow you aside from monetary interest?
- Costly services may require scene development. Training, for instance, implies the passing of time and invites the envisioning of a montage. How does it go? How does the world change as you dedicate time to develop new skills? If you have an active campaign clock, advance it.

# TAKING A HIATUS
A ==**Hiatus Stage**== is an optional structured approach for resolving an extended period of time. In it, your character is recuperating from actively undertaking the campaign while some latent threat is still developing in the background.

A hiatus stage is not a retirement from your adventures, but a preparation phase before the push. To start the hiatus stage, make the following move.

## ENTER DOWNTIME
**When you have at least one active campaign clock that threatens a vow (formidable or greater) and you spend extended time in a place safe enough to recuperate, profit, and develop your legacy,** determine the amount of time you are committing to the hiatus. Then, as appropriate to that time, set the rank of the profit progress track and a number of segments for the downtime’s tension clock.
- Half a season: Dangerous rank / four segments
- A full season: Formidable rank / eight segments
When the downtime tension clock is full, you must **Depart to Quest**.
___

Start by identifying which campaign clocks represent forces that work against you. At least one of them should signify a menace to a vow of rank formidable, extreme, or epic. If you don’t have a campaign clock that represents an active threat in your story, you can’t make this move.

As part of this move, you set...
- A profit progress track with a dangerous or formidable rank. This represents the savings that you make as you work during your hiatus.
- A downtime tension clock with four or eight segments. This represents the time left before the hiatus ends.

Both the progress track rank and the tension clock segments counter each other and are determined by the hiatus duration. When making the **Enter Downtime** move, envision what out- of-control situation lead you to this point. Were you physically or mentally at rock bottom? Were you betrayed or facing a crisis of confidence? What forces you to recuperate, improve, and overcome the crisis?

Once the hiatus is underway, **Work Your Legacy** and **Work the Field** (page 232). You will not make any other moves during this stage. Consider this approach as a zoomed out perspective in which you will only use these two moves to resolve the scenarios that make up this time period.

The choice between the two moves is a narrative decision that provides you with different long-term bonuses.
- **Work Your Legacy** represents dedicating time to your character’s growth and evolution. With this move, you will mark progress on your legacy tracks.
- **Work the Field** signifies making efforts for material gain. It leverages the Wealth and Treasure guidelines from ***Sundered Isles*** on page 28, or the ones in the **Exchange Wealth** section of this chapter on page 229. With this move, you will increase the chances of having wealth in your ledger once you **Depart to Quest**.

The decision as to which move to make will always be based on your interest and not be restricted by any other condition. Whatever your call is, one thing must be kept in mind: the clock is ticking and the cost of the downtime is that the forces opposing you will also gain advantage.

As you envision your character taking these actions, be mindful about the commitment to the downtime. Avoid getting out of it and resolving a scene with any other move. You may detail moments spent in downtime as long as it doesn’t imply taking a course of action that would derail you from the hiatus stage. However, if you decide to terminate the downtime because of a narrative outcome, make the **Depart to Quest** move and proceed with the scene.

> You **Work Your Legacy** by researching the lore of a faction in an archive and score a miss. As a result, you envision the place being taken by a higher force. Because your character is in hiatus and not taking risks, you don’t **Compel** the gatekeeper or **Face Danger** to resolve the situation. Instead, you take this impediment to research as the cost to pay—as per the miss outcome—and continue with your downtime. However, if you wish to...
> - **Compel** or **Face Danger** now, **Depart to Quest** first.
> - Take actions later on regarding the entity that took the archive, you make a note of it for when you **Depart to Quest**.

**Hiatus With Allies**
If you are playing with allies, all players enter the hiatus stage at the same time. For this, all players must have a campaign clock that opposes the fulfillment of their vows or one that works against a shared vow.

Each player uses their own profit progress tracks and downtime tension clocks. Take turns to give everyone a chance to work their legacies or fields. Prioritize those whose tension clock has the least segments filled.

## WORK YOUR LEGACY
**When you leverage your downtime to develop your abilities, improve your resources, gain a reward, or boost your influence,** envision the challenges you overcome to acquire or improve assets for your campaign. If you
focus on…
- Training for your quest: Roll +edge or +iron
- Fostering your reputation for your bonds: Roll +heart or +shadow
- Researching lore for your discoveries: Roll +wits
- 
**On a strong hit**, mark one tick on the appropriate legacy track. On a strong hit with a match, mark two ticks instead.

**On a weak hit**, mark one tick on the appropriate legacy track and fill a clock segment.

**On a miss**, fill a clock segment. On a miss with a match, fill two segments instead.
___

This is the move for character progression. Make it to mark progress on your legacy tracks. The translation of this exercise into experience allows you to acquire or upgrade assets.

When you perform this move, envision your character going through the process of bolstering their presence in or understanding of the galaxy. If they are committing to physical training, how are they achieving it? If they are fostering their reputation, how they are gaining renown? If they are researching, what discoveries are they making? Imagine the process as a montage of preparation and growth. On a miss, time has not been used as expected to make significant
advancement, and time is pressing.

If you are in the hiatus stage with allies and make this move, you may work together to envision all present characters in the montage. However, only the player making the move gets the mechanical outcome and the highlight of the envisioning. The rest of the players make their own move. The same principle applies to **Work the Field**.

## WORK THE FIELD
**When you use your downtime to profit or acquire something of worth,** envision the labor. If your work mainly consists of…
- Exerting physical grace or using your dexterity: Roll +edge
- Working with people or using your charisma: Roll +heart
- Exercising force or using your brawn: Roll +iron
- Duping people or using your guile: Roll +shadow
- Deciphering truths or using your intelligence: Roll +wits

**On a hit**, your work efficiency allows you to save for your adventures. Add +1 on your next move (not a progress move).

**On a strong hit**, mark progress. **On a strong hit with a match**, mark progress twice.

**On a weak hit**, mark progress and fill a clock segment. If you fill a profit progress track, you may set another and continue marking progress.

**On a miss**, you encounter setbacks during your routine and do not save; fill a clock segment. On a miss with a match, fill two segments instead.
___

Make this move to gain wealth by leveraging one or multiple profit progress tracks when you make the **Measure Profit** progress move right after you **Depart to Quest**.

Wealth is the currency used in Chapter 7: Special Companion Assets (page 198), Chapter 8: Item Assets (page 208), and when making the **Exchange Wealth** move (page 229). To learn more about wealth, see page 28 of ***Sundered Isles***. Otherwise, follow the guidelines in the **Exchanging Wealth** section of this chapter on page 226.

When you perform this move, envision your character working on bolstering their treasury. On a hit, you make enough for the savings and you feel emboldened to continue with your hiatus. On a miss, saving becomes an arduous endeavor.

## DEPART TO QUEST
**When you prepare to take off to continue your adventures after taking downtime,** fill segments in the campaign clocks per the hiatus duration.
- Half a season: Two segments
- A full season: Four segments
Then, do the following:
- Clear your misfortunes and any battered impacts.
- Set any condition meters to their max values.
- Set your momentum to its reset value.
Then, envision what each marked segment represents and how the world has changed during your absence. If you have progress in your profit progress track, **Measure Profit**.
___

When the tension clock is filled or your hiatus stage has been derailed, time is up. You must **Depart to Quest**. If you have made progress in one or multiple profit progress tracks by making the **Work the Field** move, make the **Measure Profit** progress move now.

Note that you don't **Depart to Quest** when a profit progress track is filled. Instead, as time is in your favor, you may continue making the **Work the Field** move to mark progress on another profit progress track. **Depart to Quest** when the downtime tension clock is filled or the narrative forces you to get back to the adventure.

As a consequence of going through a hiatus stage, you clear the slate for your character. Mechanically, you reset your misfortunes and battered impacts, as well as your condition meters. Narratively, your character is fresh and recommitted to their purpose.

Once downtime has finished, you are renewed and your vows call you back to your adventures, but your absence has given the threat that opposes you an opening. They have continued to push forward. What awaits you? How will you handle the new challenges? Who accompanies you in facing these new phases of the threats?

## MEASURE PROFIT
*Progress Move*

**When you take note of the fruits of your labor**, roll the challenge dice and compare to your progress.

**On a hit**, envision and record a ledger item with a wealth value per the hiatus duration and hit result.

| Hiatus Duration | On a Strong Hit | On a Weak Hit |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| Half a season | 3 value | 2 value |
| A full season | 5 value | 3 value |

**On a strong hit with a match**, record an additional ledger item with a value equivalent to a weak hit.

**On a miss**, the result of your labors do not translate into anything of value, and you become aware of some unanticipated complication or realization. **Pay the Price**.
___

Make this move only if you have progress in a profit progress track through the Work the Field move. When doing so, make independent progress rolls per each progress track.

On a hit, envision the item you record in your ledger and how you save it, either as a part of your equipment or stored within your ship. This item might be "payment in credits", "stockpiles", or "goods". Each item acquired through this move is recorded independently and has its own wealth value. On a miss, a complication arises.

Wealth is the used in Chapter 7: Special Companion Assets (page 198), Chapter 8: Item Assets (page 208), and with the **Exchange Wealth** move (page 229). To learn more about this currency, see page 28 of ***Sundered Isles*** or the **Exchanging Wealth** section of this chapter on page 226.

Taking inventory of your savings is the last step at the end of your hiatus stage. Now, you are returning to the adventure better prepared with resources to meet the challenges that stand between you and the fulfillment of your vows. Hiring services, acquiring items, and maintaining your ship is a more feasible task. How else will you use your wealth? How will you go about securing it?

# FAILING FORWARD
Facing hardships and overcoming them is a key part of any adventure. These are keystones of your character growth. If you would like to make failures an influential aspect of your character progression, use the following guidelines. You can introduce this mechanic into an ongoing campaign or a new one.

First, create a ==**failure track**==. This is a standard progress track with ten boxes. Then, when you make an action or progress roll and score a miss, make the following move.

## MARK YOUR FAILURE
**When you make a move and score a miss,** you may mark progress on your failure track per the table below.

| ------------ | ------------Outcome ---------- |
| :---: | :---: |

| Roll | Miss | Miss With a Match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action roll | One tick | Two ticks |
| Progress roll | Two ticks | One box |
___

Don't **Mark Your Failure** if you change the outcome of a move from a miss to a hit through an asset ability or other means. Make this move only when you face the consequences of a miss.

Continue to mark ticks on your failure track as you play and score misses. When your failure track is at +6 filled progress boxes or greater, you may perform the **Learn From Your Failures** progress move.

## LEARN FROM YOUR FAILURES
*Progress Move*

**When you spend time reflecting on your hardships and missteps, and your failure track is +6 or greater,** roll your challenge dice and compare to your progress.

**On a strong hit**, you commit to a dramatic change. Mark one box on your quests legacy track, clear all progress on the failure track, and choose one.
- Change course: Discard a single asset, take 2 experience for each marked ability, and **Advance** to spend this experience on new assets or upgrades.
- Make it right: **Swear an Iron Vow** and reroll any dice.
- Ready your next steps: Take +3 momentum.

**On a weak hit**, you learn from your mistakes. Mark two ticks on your quests legacy track and clear all progress on the failure track.

**On a miss**, you’ve learned the wrong lessons. Mark 1 tick on your quests legacy track and clear all progress on the failure track. Then, envision how you set off on an ill-fated path.
___

**Learn From Your Failures** is the progress move to resolve the impact of character's mishaps. Make this move when you are ready to leverage the misses you have marked thus far. The failure track must be at +6 progress or more.

Narratively, your character is reflecting on the consequences of their failures. Their adversities have brought them to this point. What have you learned? How do you intend to correct your ways?

When making the progress roll, consider the outcome within the narrative. On a strong hit, this is a significant turning point in your character’s story. On a weak hit, you grudgingly accept the lessons you draw from the failures, or those lessons are limited. On a miss, you haven't learned enough, or your pride prevents you from making a connection that will lead to real growth.

Whatever the outcome of the progress move, clear all progress on the failure track and return to the loop: make the **Mark Your Failure** move whenever you score a miss and **Learn from Your Failures** once your failure track is +6 or greater.

**Failure Track & Allies**

The failure track is not a shared resource. If you are playing with allies, each of you has your own progress track to mark your own misses with the **Mark Your Failure** move.

In case of making the **Aid Your Ally** move and the outcome is a miss, do whatever fits the narrative. If you are close collaborators in the action, you both **Mark Your Failure** to represent the shared learning experience.

When an ally makes the **Learn from Your Failures** move in the presence of others, envision how they participate in the scene. Is there an exchange of words? Are there reproaches for mistakes made? Does this change their bonds in any way? If so, how?

# REWORKING YOUR LEGACY
Given that ***Ancient Wonders*** incorporates special companions and item assets within the ***Starforged*** system, the legacy moves—except **Earn Experience**—have undergone some adjustments.

## ADVANCE
**When you develop your abilities, improve your resources, gain a reward, or boost your influence,** you may spend experience to add a new asset or upgrade one. Choose from the following categories as appropriate to your focus and opportunities.

| Asset Type | Cost to Acquire | Cost to Upgrade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vehicle, module, path, deed, or companion | 3 | 2 |
| Special companion | 4 | 3 |
| Item | 1 | 1 |
___

This revision of the **Advance** move includes special companions, item assets, and the possibility of upgrading the character with the wealth resource, which inclusion in the system is explained in the **Exchanging Wealth** section of this chapter, on page 226.

An alternative to the **Advance** moves for character progression is the **Exchange Wealth** move on page 229, which uses the currency resource instead of experience.

## CONTINUE A LEGACY
*Progress Move*

**When you retire from your life as Ironsworn, or succumb to death or desolation,** you may create a new character in your established setting. If you do, roll the challenge dice and compare the results of the former character’s legacy tracks: quests, bonds, and discoveries (one roll per track).

For each **strong hit**, choose one from below, or one from the weak hit or miss options.
- Follow their path: Take one path or companion asset (regular or special) from the former character (at no cost), including any marked abilities.
- Share a connection: Take one connection from the former character, including any accrued progress or bond benefits.
- Accept an inheritance: Take the former character’s command vehicle and one module or support vehicle, as well as an additional item (at no cost), including any marked abilities.

For each **weak hit**, choose one from below, or one from the miss options.
- See it through: Choose one of the former character’s unfinished quests, and **Swear an Iron Vow** (with an automatic strong hit) to see it done. You may immediately mark up to half the former character's earned progress (round down) on this quest.
- Rebuild a connection: Name one of the former character’s connections, and envision how time or circumstances have changed them in a dramatic way. When you **Make a Connection** with them, take an automatic strong hit and mark two ticks on your bonds legacy track.
- Explore familiar ground: Name a location that was meaningful to the former character. When you first visit that place, envision how it has changed or is endangered. Then, mark two ticks on your discoveries legacy track.

For each **miss**, choose one.
- Deal with the aftermath: Envision how one of your former character’s foes has gained power or influence.
- Switch loyalties: Envision how you begin in opposition to your former character’s beliefs, goals, or allegiances.
- Open Pandora’s Box: Envision how an advancement or discovery has unleashed unexpectedly dire consequences.

*237 CHAPTER 9: ADDITIONAL MOVES*

==Ancient Wonders== is a system-agnostic, modular expansion based on ==Ironsworn: Starforged==, the science fiction tabletop roleplaying game by Shawn Tomkin.

Throughout this supplement, you will be presented with a plethora of random tables to enhance your experience in the science fiction game of your preference. ***Ancient Wonders*** offers...
- A realistic ==solar system generator== for planetary and asteroid composition. This sub-system provides more richness in exploration and a more concisely focused narrative experience.
- An expansive ==planet generator== with a great variety of world classes and random tables to detail them, as well as oracles to create encounters on planetary surfaces and underwater.
- ==Megastructures== to discover, with a set of oracles and a sub-system for undertaking extensive expeditions in the alien, unfathomable depths of these marvels.
- ==Megacities== to explore, with a set of oracles and a sub-system for researching the alien societies that once inhabited them
- ==Construct==, ==bio-mechanic==, and ==anomaly generators== for encountering them in the most hazardous sites.
- ==Special companions== as a new type of asset for the most challenging adventures. Made for Starforged.
- ==Items== as a new type of assets to loot, find, and trade in the galaxy. Made for Starforged.
- ==Additional moves== for making camp, entering into a hiatus stage, exchanging wealth, and failing forward in your character progression. Made for Starforged.
