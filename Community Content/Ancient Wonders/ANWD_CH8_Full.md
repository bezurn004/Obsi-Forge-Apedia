# ADVANCED GEAR
By default, ***Starforged*** does not focus on equipment. Your character's gear is abstracted in the supply value, and the objects you carry serve as narrative tools to influence your story. However...
- The **Check Your Gear** move (***Starforged*** rulebook, page 153) introduces particular items in your pack that you can use to overcome challenges.
- Some assets are object-centric, such as GUNSLINGER, which requires wielding a pistol, while other assets are vehicles or modules.
- ***Sundered Isles*** expands the concept of item management by introducing the treasury and wealth mechanic (page 28).

***Ancient Wonders*** expands the system by offering a larger item repertoire in the form of item assets. Just like regular assets, items are formatted as printable or preprinted cards.

## ITEM ASSET TYPES
Besides vehicles and modules, ***Ancient Wonders*** adds the four types of items listed below.
- ==**Apparel**== must be worn to function.
- ==**Consumables**== are limited use items. If a consumable has been upgraded enough that it is no longer consumed on use, its type changes to one more appropriate.
- ==**Tools**== are gadgets made for a particular purpose. They are usually hand-held but may function as wearables.
- ==**Weapons**== are items designed to inflict harm or cause damage.

## ITEM RARITIES
***Ancient Wonders*** outlines items into five rarities.
- **Common** [C]: Simple objects, such as food, water, fuel, regular weapons, ammo, and general upkeep. They are mundane pieces of equipment and provisions on hand and are implicit in the personal and hold supplies. These items do not have mechanical benefits, but instead satisfy narrative prerequisites.
- **Uncommon** [U]: Fine objects. This rarity and higher are characterized by either having a quality that stands out from the objects abstracted in the supply value or because they directly interact with the game mechanics.
- **Notable** [N]: Exceptional objects of above-average value. They are improved versions of uncommon objects.
- **Rare** [R] : Precious items. Support vehicles and modules belong to this rarity due to their cost.
- **Exotic** [E] : Wondrous objects of the highest value. By default, these objects are of alien origin, although some might be human-made according to exceptional narrative circumstances. Command vehicles, for instance, belong to this rarity.

## PRECURSOR ITEMS
By default, the rarity of precursor objects is handled with a "one tier higher" rule of thumb in comparison to human items.

| Precursor item | Human value |
| :---: | :---: |
| Common | Uncommon |
| Uncommon | Notable |
| Notable | Rare |
| Rare | Exotic |

# USING ITEM ASSETS
Each ability in an ***Ancient Wonders*** item represents a rarity. When you have an item of a certain rarity, you also get the boons of the previous ones. For example, if you have a rare item, you also have unlocked the abilities of the  uncommon and notable rarities.

Just like regular assets, the boons of the abilities stack and can be used simultaneously. Each ability assumes the boon of the previous ones. When the boon of a latter ability overlaps, it is a replacement. When it doesn't overlap, it is an addition.

## BROKEN ITEMS
There are two instances in which you may have a broken item on your hands.
- When you acquire an item in less-than-ideal circumstances. This might be because you found it discarded in an expedition site or because the merchant that traded it with you did not keep the object in a good state. If unsure **Ask the Oracle**.
- When you **Pay the Price**, you can mark an item as broken as the cost.

To indicate that an item is broken, flip the card over or use a marker on it. A broken item cannot be used until you successfully make the Repair move (***Starforged Reference Guide***, page 21) and spend...
- 3 repair points for human-made items
- 5 repair points for precursor-made items

## ADDING AN EXOTIC RARITY
Not all items have an exotic ability. If you wish for an object to have one, follow these guidelines:
- Increase the effect of the previous ability by one in terms of numbers or narrative impact.
- Do not make an ability provide a +3 or greater on a roll. Instead, recur to increasing the effect of another ability.
- Make the narrative condition to use the item less restrictive.

## ITEM USES
If an item has a meter with no 0 value, it means that its uses cannot be replenished until the object is acquired again. Once all uses have been consumed, the item is discarded.

## ITEMS ACQUISITION AND UPGRADE
This section guides you through the process of acquiring and upgrading non-common items.

## LOOTING ITEMS
To loot items, use a dedicated deck of item assets and follow these guidelines:
- If a situation gives you the chance of finding an item by making a move, you may do so. Then, roll on the Item Rarity table below. Examples of such cases are...
	- **Resupply** with a strong hit with a match.
	- **Explore a Waypoint** and choosing to find an opportunity.
	- **Make a Discovery** and choosing to find a device or artifact.
- Draw item cards from the deck until you get one with the resulting rarity, or choose one as the narrative dictates.
- The looted item is in a broken state unless you roll a match when rolling on the table.

### ITEM RARITY

| 64—88 | 16—65 | Notable |
| 89—98 | 66—90 | Rare |
| 99—100 | 91—100 | Exotic |

## BUYING ITEMS
You may acquire items in exchange for wealth or experience by making the **Exchange Wealth** move (page 229) or **Advance** move (page 236). When doing so, pay the cost based on the rarity.

| Rarity | Experience Cost | Wealth Cost |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| Uncommon | 1 | 2 |
| Notable | 2 | 3 |
| Rare | 3 | 4 |
| Exotic | 4 | 5 |

Wealth might be obtained by **Taking a Hiatus**—a set of moves on page 230—as well as by other means explained on page 28 of **Sundered Isles**. Other uses for currency are found in the **Exchange Wealth** move on page 229.

## IMPROVING ITEMS
You may also upgrade items by making the **Exchange Wealth** and **Advance** ***Ancient Wonders*** moves on pages 229 and 236 respectively. If you...
- **Exchange Wealth** to improve an item: Pay 2 wealth
- **Exchange Wealth** to improve a vehicle or module: Pay 3 wealth
- **Advance** to improve an item: Pay 1 experience
- **Advance** to improve a vehicle or module: Pay 2 experience

# SPLINTERS
The most mysterious and powerful objects are not artifacts found in a glass case or on a pedestal, but at the core of the strangest, most alien locations. It was not until after numerous expeditions and failed encounters with anomalies that, after finally dispelling one, humanity came upon relics of powerful, esoteric energy.

==**Splinters**== are items from eons ago that have survived the crushing passage of time and are presumably the cause of the anomalies that emerged in the depths of alien sites. Given their properties, the goal of many explorers of ancient, alien ruins is to acquire these priceless objects of power.

Expert delvers to have obtaned splinters claim that their tiny size is deceiving. They bestow great power on their wearers, but only temporarily; they are prone to fading from existence once their energy has been harnessed. Additionally, splinters cannot exist for long without contact with some life form.

Some delvers remark on splinters' subjective effects as well. According to these explorers, there is a special thrill that comes with using these alien objects as well as a burning desire to gain their power. Everyone agrees that using a splinter feels as if one is closer to what they have come to understand as the physical and cognitive capabilities of the precursors, but they advise against carrying many of these items, as they become intolerable for reasons yet unknown.

## ACQUIRING SPLINTERS
SPLINTERS are special assets in the form of blank cards. You don't include them in your item deck, nor do you acquire them with the **Advance** move. Instead, you acquire a splinter by successfully finishing an encounter by overcoming an aberration or entity, as explained on the **Anomalies** section of page 194. When overcome, whether by defeating an entity or directly seizing the splinter at the core of the aberration, these anomalies leave the item behind.
- An entity holds a minor splinter
- An aberration holds major splinter

When acquiring a splinter, use the oracles on pages 220-221 to reveal its properties. Mark each oracle result on a SPLINTER card. Then, name it. You may choose a name appropriate to the nature of the artifact or the anomaly that left it behind. For inspiration, you may use any combination of oracles, such as Descriptor + Theme (pages 12—13).

Alternatively, you can pregenerate splinters. ***Ancient Wonders*** comes with 12 SPLINTER blank cards for you fill and make a deck of them.

Finding splinters is one of the most addictive of pursuits for a delver. Explorers claim that the excitement of discovering new, unique, and esoteric artifacts is what drives most of their daring expeditions.

### BUYING SPLINTERS
Splinters may also be acquired through the **Exchange Wealth** move on page 229. When doing so, envision the nature of the trade. What is the merchant's reaction to your inquiry about such a rare artifact? How are they keeping the alien relic safe? What policies does the community have regarding these items?

## USING SPLINTERS
When you generate a splinter with the oracles present on pages 220-221, you mark two key properties to use it.
- ==**Focus:**== This is the aspect of your character that the splinter affects when used. You may associate a focus with a stat, but not restrictively. Just like its *Shape* and *Material*, when determining the splinter's *Focus*, you may either roll on the table or choose one result appropriate to the anomaly the splinter came from.
- ==**Bonus:**== This is the mechanical advantage you get when using the splinter through a move. There are two types of bonuses—minor and major. If the item was dropped by an entity, use the *Minor Splinter Bonus* oracle; if the item was dropped by an aberration, use the *Major Splinter Bonus* oracle instead.

When you make a move using the focus, decide before rolling if you are leveraging the splinter’s power. Keep in mind that these objects are very volatile; when you use a splinter and roll a 6 on your action die, the item disappears after resolving the move. In this case, discard the asset. If you decide to use its power, make the move and apply its bonus. Envision the effect: how does the power of the relic manifest? How do the witnesses and environment react to it?

If a SPLINTER offers bonuses for different outcomes, you take all the bonuses that apply even after the outcome changes. For instance, if a splinter offers you a benefit on a miss and on a hit, and you score a miss, take the miss benefit. If that results in you improving your result to a hit, also take the hit benefit as well.

## THE SPLINTERS' BURDEN
Carrying more than two splinters bears an inexplicable burden. Some people experience it as bad luck, others as a spiritual malaise.

Having more than two SPLINTER assets counts as an amount of impacts equal to the amount of additional splinter. Carrying 3 splinters count as 1 impact (-1 max momentum), 4 splinters count as 2 impacts (-2 max momentum), and so on.

This burden is suffered individually, and not per the total of splinters carried by the party members, you and your allies.

Impacts marked this way cannot be cleared by anything but by discarding any additional SPLINTERS.
