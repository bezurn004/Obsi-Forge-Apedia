# [[_Ancient Wonders\|Ancient Wonders]] - [[_ANWD_CH2_Solar-Systems|Solar Systems]]: A Guide to Finding Solar Systems
Since the first stellar voyages, mankind has paved its way through the cosmos by navigating to the light of distant stars. The content present in this chapter is based on the assumption that space travel is not accomplished by blindly entering absolute darkness, but by letting stellar objects guide our course; a course that leads us to encounter solar systems made up of an infinite variety of compositions.

In **_Starforged_**, sectors are unmeasured areas with undetermined sizes that may hold a variety of objects, including stars, planets, and many other space sightings.

Expanding on that idea, the following subsystem further solidifies what a sector is by redefining its area to that of a solar system. Here, you will find a more structured approach to sector creation and instructions on how to generate plausible worlds. Planetary classes are determined by proximity to a stellar object.

Finally, this system also includes two new maps, simple and detailed, upon which to showcase and mark the many celestial bodies that make up a solar system.

## Key Terms And Concepts
The following are the key concepts used in this chapter, their corresponding term definitions, and how some aspects of the system interact with your game.

### Solar System
In this chapter, a sector is instead referred to as a solar system. A solar system is a collection of celestial bodies, ranging from the central stellar object to planets, asteroid belts, and other sightings.

For practical purposes, these two terms will be used interchangeably. If you are using this chapter for your game sessions and you see the term "sector", it is also referring to a solar system.

### Stellar Object
==Stellar objects== are located at the heart of any solar system and are orbited by a variety of objects; from planets, to asteroid belts and various space sightings.

When you use the _Stellar Object_ oracle, instead of the ***Starforged*** table, use the one on page 27.

### Orbits
The term ==orbit== refers to the locations where planets and asteroid belts are marked. They are always counted outwards from the stellar object.

There are two types of orbit:

- ==Planetary orbits:== These are the locations where planets can be generated and discovered. There are six (6) in total in a solar system.
- ==Interplanetary orbits:== These are the locations where asteroid belts can be generated and discovered. There are five (5) in total in a solar system, and they are placed between the planetary orbits.

### Asteroid Belts
==Asteroid belts== are collections of asteroids that encircle a stellar object. It is assumed that they consist of the remains of previously destroyed planets or planets that were never able to successfully form.

### Heat Map
Each stellar object has an associated ==heat map==. A heat map is a table that shows the temperature of each planetary orbit in a solar system based on the stellar object. These temperatures range from searing to frigid.

As a result of the temperature caused by the stellar object, the worlds that orbit it will have varying chances of belonging to different planetary classes.

### Space Sightings
Given that the solar system generator determines both the stellar object and the placements of planets, these objects can no longer be discovered at random via the _[[ANWD_CH2_Asteroid-Clusters-Space-Sightings#Oracle Space Sightings|Space Sightings]]_ oracle. Instead of using the **_Starforged_** table, use the one on page 27.

### Information In Advance
During your exploration of the galaxy, you may obtain information about any given solar system before traveling there. This may give you an immediate insight into that area of space.

You may get information in advance for any of the following:

- Stellar object
- Number of planets and their orbits
- Planetary information
- Asteroid belts
- Passages

For any object that would not necessarily appear in a fixed location, such as a debris field or starship, you may either...

- Mark it at a location that would best suit your current narrative
- Use the _[[ANWD_CH2_Sector-Sheets#Coordinates|Coordinate]]_ oracle on page 19

When you have information in advance about a solar system, you may make a note of the data or mark it on either a simple or detailed sector sheet as shown in the next couple of pages.
